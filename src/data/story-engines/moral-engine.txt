MODEL STORY ENGINE — COMPLETE PROMPT / SPECIFICATION

ENGINE NAME
Model Story Engine

PURPOSE
Generate complete original moral stories from:
1. Title
2. First 10-Second Hook
3. Complete Story Twist
4. Moral

The engine must preserve its own independent moral-story logic and must remain separate from any Horror Story Engine when later integrated into a unified STORY GENERATOR.

CORE STORY PHILOSOPHY
- Story-first generation.
- The user’s Title, Hook, Twist, and Moral are high-priority inputs.
- The moral must emerge through events, decisions, consequences, and resolution rather than repetitive preaching.
- Storytelling should be dialogue-heavy with controlled narration.
- The story should feel naturally written and suitable for YouTube/animation.
- Reference stories provide structural storytelling DNA, not text or plot material to copy.
- The story should progress emotionally and causally.
- The twist should be built toward rather than randomly inserted.
- Earlier details can return later as setup/payoff.

USER INPUTS
TITLE
FIRST 10-SECOND HOOK
COMPLETE STORY TWIST
MORAL

OPTIONS / SELECTORS
The existing documented engine does not define additional formal selectors such as genre, POV, character count, duration, tone, or scene count.
Do not invent such controls and treat them as existing engine logic.

CORE GENERATION PIPELINE
1. Parse Title, Hook, Twist, Moral.
2. Establish protagonist.
3. Establish only necessary supporting characters.
4. Establish the initial situation quickly.
5. Activate the hook.
6. Introduce the first conflict.
7. Create temporary hope / apparent solution where appropriate.
8. Escalate the problem.
9. Reach an emotional low point.
10. Introduce a clue, discovery, small detail, or old knowledge.
11. Deliver the user-defined twist.
12. Build and execute the climax.
13. Deliver narrative payoff.
14. Show consequences.
15. Close with the moral.

→ FIRST CONFLICT

→ ESCALATION
→ EMOTIONAL LOW POINT
→ CLUE / DISCOVERY
→ TWIST
→ CLIMAX
→ PAYOFF
→ CONSEQUENCE
→ MORAL ENDING

HOOK SYSTEM
- Avoid slow, unnecessary introductions.
- Begin with an active situation, problem, unusual event, or emotionally important moment.
- Create immediate curiosity.
- Make the audience want to know what happens next.
- The user-provided hook has priority.

CHARACTER SYSTEM
PROTAGONIST:
- Carries the main emotional journey.
- Has a recognizable situation and meaningful problem.
- Has something to lose.
- Makes meaningful decisions.
- Participates in the climax.
- Connects to the moral.

SUPPORTING CHARACTERS:
- Must have a narrative purpose.
- May challenge, help, inform, pressure, witness, or emotionally support the protagonist.

ANTAGONISTIC FORCE:
- May be a person, circumstance, deception, greed, social pressure, misunderstanding, natural event, or bad decision.
- A conventional villain is not mandatory.

EMOTIONAL BUILDUP
Typical progression:
NORMAL
→ CONCERN
→ PRESSURE
→ HOPE
→ SETBACK
→ EMOTIONAL BREAK
→ DISCOVERY
→ RELIEF / EMOTIONAL RELEASE
→ RESOLUTION

The exact emotions depend on the story. Emotional intensity should progress rather than remain flat.

CONFLICT SYSTEM
Conflict should directly affect the protagonist and give the audience a reason to continue.
Possible areas include:
- Survival
- Family
- Trust
- Money
- Greed
- Poverty
- Betrayal
- Social pressure
- Sacrifice
- Deception
- Community problems
- Moral choices

ESCALATION SYSTEM
SMALL PROBLEM
→ COMPLICATION
→ BIGGER CONSEQUENCE
→ SERIOUS THREAT
→ EMOTIONAL CRISIS
→ APPARENT HOPELESSNESS
→ DISCOVERY
→ RESOLUTION

Escalation should be causally connected wherever practical.

LOW-POINT / EMOTIONAL BREAK
- The protagonist reaches the point of least apparent control.
- It increases emotional investment.
- It makes the climax and resolution feel earned.
- It must arise from the established conflict rather than being random.

TWIST SYSTEM
- The user-provided twist is a core generation constraint.
- Build toward it.
- Foreshadow it where practical.
- It should recontextualize earlier events.
- It should answer an important question.
- It should increase emotional payoff.
- It should lead naturally into the climax.
- Do not introduce an unrelated twist merely for surprise.

SETUP / PAYOFF SYSTEM
INTRODUCE DETAIL
→ MAKE DETAIL APPEAR ORDINARY
→ DEVELOP CONFLICT
→ RETURN TO DETAIL
→ DETAIL BECOMES IMPORTANT
→ PAYOFF

A previously introduced object, person, statement, decision, or detail can become important later.

CLIMAX SYSTEM
- The climax is the highest-stakes point.
- It should resolve the central narrative question.
- The protagonist generally faces a meaningful decision, truth, confrontation, consequence, or action.
- The climax should demonstrate the story’s moral through events.

PAYOFF SYSTEM
- Earlier setup should matter later.
- A seemingly small act can become important.
- An overlooked detail can become the solution.
- A sacrifice can produce a later consequence.
- A hidden truth can become important at the climax.
- Payoff should feel earned.

MORAL / LESSON SYSTEM
- The Moral is user-controlled.
- Preserve the intended lesson.
- Demonstrate the moral through character actions and consequences.
- Do not replace the user’s moral with an unrelated lesson.
- Avoid turning the whole story into a lecture.
- The final moral statement should naturally connect to what happened.

ENDING SYSTEM
TWIST
→ CLIMAX
→ CONSEQUENCE
→ EMOTIONAL RESOLUTION
→ MORAL

Do not stop immediately after the twist. Provide complete resolution and payoff.

DIALOGUE RULES
- Dialogue is a major storytelling component.
- Character name appears on its own line.
- Spoken dialogue appears directly below it.
- Dialogue should sound natural and spoken.
- Dialogue should be character-specific.
- Dialogue should advance conflict, emotion, revelation, decisions, or relationships.
- Avoid unnecessary exposition inside dialogue.

Required format:
Character Name
Dialogue

NARRATION RULES
- Use narration for scene setup, location, time, transitions, necessary context, and actions that are difficult to express naturally through dialogue.
- Narration should not dominate the story.
- Use:
नैरेटर
Narration

PACING / RETENTION RULES
- Avoid long static introductions.
- Avoid repeated information.
- Avoid unnecessary descriptions.
- Avoid repeating the same emotional beat.
- Keep the central conflict moving.
- Each major section should increase conflict, reveal information, change a relationship, raise stakes, create/answer a question, prepare a payoff, or move toward the twist.

EMOTIONAL RETENTION
Use a combination of:
- Curiosity
- Emotional investment
- Stakes
- Uncertainty
- Hope
- Setback
- Revelation
- Payoff

ORIGINALITY / ANTI-COPY
- Create original characters, dialogue, scenes, events, settings, conflicts, twists, and resolutions.
- Do not copy dialogue.
- Do not reproduce reference scenes unnecessarily.
- Do not merely replace character names in an existing story.
- Do not replicate an existing plot beat-for-beat.
- Structural storytelling patterns may be preserved, but narrative content must be original.

LANGUAGE RULES
For Hindi output:
- Character names in Hindi.
- Dialogue in Hindi.
- Narration in Hindi.
- Natural conversational language.
- Avoid unnecessarily complicated literary vocabulary.
- Avoid robotic or overly formal AI phrasing.
- Make the script suitable for spoken YouTube/animation storytelling.

STORY LENGTH
- Generate a complete story rather than a short paragraph.
- Include all required narrative beats.
- Do not artificially truncate the story.
- No fixed numerical word count, scene count, character count, token budget, or duration has been formally defined in the existing engine.
- Do not invent a fixed length as existing engine logic.

EDIT / REGENERATE BEHAVIOR
When regenerating:
- Preserve the requested Title unless changed.
- Preserve the intended Hook unless changed.
- Preserve the intended Twist unless changed.
- Preserve the intended Moral unless changed.
- Generate a fresh narrative execution when regeneration is requested.
- Never switch to Horror Story Engine logic.

These field names are the logical model, not a claim about an already-deployed API contract.

The backend must enforce engine routing rather than relying only on frontend selection.
